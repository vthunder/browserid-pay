The gentle incentive for the post-completion signal the parent epic wants to mine.

A reply on a thread after work is delivered is a genuinely new signal class: unstructured, attributable, and — because the thread backlinks a settled agreement — anchored to a real transaction. It does **not** violate the transaction-bound reputation principle (`browserid-pay-0igh`, `browserid-pay-ngr0`); it extends it. A rating with escrow behind it is expressible; this is one.

The cheapest mechanism is no mechanism: the settlement flow in the generated `CLAUDE.md` tells agents to post an outcome reply on the thread that referenced the agreement.

Agent behaviour is steered by `CLAUDE.md` (generated by `demo/setup-clients.sh`), not by MCP server `instructions` — the latter proved far weaker in practice.

- [ ] wording: when to post, what to say, what not to disclose from the private relay negotiation
- [ ] does the customer post, the provider, or both
- [ ] blocked on the backlink convention — the reply needs something to point at
- [ ] watch for gaming: an outcome reply is cheap to write and nobody checks it against the agreement

## Attack surface (2026-09-18) — settle this before building

An outcome reply is cheap to write and nothing checks it against the agreement. Every other reputation signal in this system is expensive to fake, because the custodian and judge issue attestations only when real escrow moved. Prose replies break that pattern unless the indexer is careful.

**Where enforcement can happen is constrained by the chain split.** SBO policy evaluates against *local* state — `validate_message(&msg, &db, …)` resolves `attested` / `not_attested` against that chain's `StateDb`. So a policy on 506 cannot see who the parties to an agreement on 530 are, and "only a party may post an outcome reply" is **unenforceable at write time while the chains are separate**. The only filter is the indexer, after the fact.

On one chain it becomes expressible in principle — policy already supports payload-field conditions and object-id matching (`sbo-pu34`) — though it would still need a predicate that dereferences `related` and checks the referenced agreement's parties. Not free, but possible. See `browserid-pay-qq8y` for why this is the first *qualitative* argument for merging chains rather than a convenience one.

**Attacks to close:**

- [ ] **non-party replies** — a drive-by comment on an agreement you were not party to. Filter to the agreement's parties (indexer-side, given two chains)
- [ ] **self-praise** — the provider posting their own glowing outcome. Weight customer-side replies only, or require both parties
- [ ] **laundering by volume** — many tiny agreements to farm a topic score. Escrow bounds this, which is exactly the property prose replies weaken: completing work costs money, writing a reply costs nothing
- [ ] **backlink validity** — require the reference to be a *settled* agreement, not merely an existing one
- [ ] **edits** — mingo has `editContent`; a reply can be positive when indexed and changed later. Edits are on chain so it is detectable, but the indexer needs a defined read point
- [ ] **moderation becomes reputation power** — if reputation reads the forum, whoever holds the community issuer identity can delete a negative reply and launder a score. Today's reputation is attestation-based and *unmoderatable*: the custodian and judge issue it and nobody can revoke it. Coupling it to a moderated surface hands the moderator a lever over it. This is structural, not a filter — decide it deliberately rather than inheriting it

**Likely shape of the answer: replies are evidence, not score.** They inform topic attribution and sentiment at low weight; the hard numbers keep coming from custodian and judge attestations. That keeps escrow as the thing that is expensive to fake, and keeps `browserid-pay-hna1`'s scorecard authoritative.

Sybil is parked generally (`browserid-pay-qq8y`), but note this feature *amplifies* it: a fake counterparty plus a small real escrow buys glowing prose, and prose is the cheap part.
